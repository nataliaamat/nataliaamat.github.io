Natalia
Amat-Lefort

I study the interaction between humans and emerging technologies, bridging behavioral science and computer science.

As a Lecturer and Researcher at Leiden University's Institute of Advanced Computer Science (LIACS), I design user studies to understand how people interact with technology.

My current work focuses on Human–AI interaction. Drawing on my background in consumer research, I use quantitative survey methods (Structural Equation Modelling) and text analytics to explore how people perceive, trust, and adopt AI. I also investigate how psychometric factors and sociodemographic differences influence the use of AI for various purposes, such as emotional support.

01 / About

About me

From Industry to Academia

Before becoming a teacher and researcher, I worked as a Senior Strategy & Operations Consultant at KPMG and EY, where I learned to build financial models and optimize operations for clients across different sectors. During this time, I volunteered to teach entrepreneurship and digital transformation courses to high-school students through Junior Achievement. These teaching experiences motivated my decision to pursue an academic career.

Research Evolution: From Digital Platforms to AI

My research investigates how humans interact with emerging technologies. During my PhD at UIC Barcelona (2019–2023), I focused on consumer studies within the sharing economy, exploring service quality and satisfaction in digital platforms like Airbnb and shared mobility networks. As technology has evolved, so has my focus. At LIACS, my work transitioned from digital platforms, to chatbots, to my current focus on human–AI interaction. In particular, my most recent work explores the adoption of Large Language Models (LLMs) for emotional support.

Methodological Approach

My work bridges behavioral science and computer science. Drawing on my background in consumer research, I design user studies to understand technology adoption and user perceptions. I use quantitative survey methodologies (such as Structural Equation Modeling) and text analytics (including topic modeling, sentiment analysis, and LLM-assisted knowledge extraction) to analyze user-generated content and prompts. Ultimately, my goal is to translate complex human–technology dynamics into actionable insights to make AI development more human-centered.

02 / Research

Research threads

01

Human–AI Interaction & Generative AI Adoption

As AI systems shift from tools to collaborative partners, understanding how users perceive and interact with AI is key. This research thread explores user perception, trust, and the behavioral drivers behind adopting conversational agents and AI systems such as LLMs. For example, my research investigates how factors like anthropomorphism (making a chatbot feel “more human”) affect user experience, and how sociodemographic differences shape the ways people adopt and trust LLMs for tasks like emotional support. The goal is to establish empirical foundations that ensure conversational systems are safe and human-centered.

Key work From Chatbots to Confidants (2026) · More Human, Less Bot (2024) View related publications →
02

Applied Language Technologies & User Text Analytics

Traditional user-research methods often struggle to scale or to capture granular sentiment from the massive volume of user-generated content available today. This ongoing pillar deploys Natural Language Processing and text analytics (from sentiment features and topic models to LLMs) to extract insights from textual data like user reviews or prompts. Recent projects include building issue-detection systems for public-sector applications (Dutch government apps) and designing privacy-preserving information-extraction methods for sensitive legal text.

Key work Int. Journal of Quality & Reliability Management (2023) · HICSS (2023) View related publications →
03

Consumer Dynamics & Experience in Digital Platforms

The rapid rise of the sharing economy and peer-to-peer digital platforms (home-sharing, shared mobility) transformed consumer expectations. This theme (main focus of my PhD research) addressed the complexities of multi-sided markets, investigating what drives customer loyalty, satisfaction, and friction. By introducing frameworks to conceptualize and measure “service inconvenience” and the alignment of guest and host expectations, this research gives digital platforms the behavioral frameworks they need to improve service quality and future-proof user retention in highly competitive ecosystems.

Key work Journal of Service Research (2026) · Int. J. of Consumer Studies (2025) View related publications →
